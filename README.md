# Pharmacovigilance Case Study: Suspected Amoxicillin–Clavulanate-Induced Liver Injury

## 1. Patient Case Summary

A 42-year-old woman weighing 58 kg presented to the hospital with nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, fatigue, and yellowish discoloration of her eyes. She had recently received amoxicillin–clavulanate for a respiratory tract infection.

Her symptoms began several days after treatment was initiated and progressively worsened. Laboratory investigations revealed elevated liver enzymes and bilirubin. The treating team suspected drug-induced liver injury and discontinued the suspected medication.

## 3. Medication History

| Medication              | Dose and frequency               |

| Paracetamol             | 500 mg orally, as needed         |             
| Multivitamin Supplement | One tablet daily                 |           


## 4. Symptoms and Timeline

* **Day 1:** Amoxicillin–clavulanate was initiated for a respiratory tract infection.
* **Day 5:** The patient developed nausea and mild vomiting.
* **Day 6:** Loss of appetite and fatigue became noticeable.
* **Day 7:** Yellowish discoloration of the eyes and skin developed.
* **Day 8:** Liver function tests showed abnormalities. The suspected antibiotic was discontinued and further evaluation was initiated.
* **Follow-up:** Symptoms and liver test results were monitored after withdrawal of the suspected drug.

## 5. Clinical Findings

The simulated examination showed jaundice and mild abdominal tenderness.

| Laboratory parameter | Illustrative result | Interpretation |
| -------------------- | ------------------: | -------------- |
| ALT                  |             412 U/L | Elevated       |
| AST                  |             356 U/L | Elevated       |
| Total bilirubin      |           3.6 mg/dL | Elevated       |
| Alkaline phosphatase |             198 U/L | Elevated       |

These values suggest liver injury but are insufficient by themselves to establish its cause or classify the injury pattern definitively. Laboratory reference ranges, baseline results, and further investigations would be required.

## 6. Identification of the Suspected ADR

**Suspected reaction:** Drug-induced liver injury (DILI), presenting with elevated liver enzymes and jaundice.

Amoxicillin–clavulanate is a recognized cause of idiosyncratic drug-induced liver injury. The timing and clinical presentation make it a plausible suspected drug in this scenario.

However, viral hepatitis, biliary obstruction, other medications, and alternative liver diseases must be investigated before attributing the reaction to the antibiotic.

## 7. Suspected Drug Assessment

**Primary suspect:** Amoxicillin–clavulanate.

The assessment is based on:

* A plausible temporal relationship between drug exposure and symptom onset.
* A clinical presentation compatible with liver injury.
* The established association between amoxicillin–clavulanate and drug-induced liver injury.

Paracetamol and other medications should also be reviewed, including the total dose consumed. No concomitant medication should be excluded as a potential contributor without sufficient information.

## 8. Causality Assessment: WHO–UMC

| Criterion             | Assessment                                              |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Temporal relationship | Compatible with a drug-related reaction                 |
| Known association     | Liver injury is a recognized adverse effect             |
| Dechallenge           | Improvement must be confirmed through follow-up records |
| Rechallenge           | Not performed                                           |
| Alternative causes    | Require investigation                                   |
| Overall assessment    | Possible ADR on the information currently available     |


## 9. Dechallenge and Rechallenge

**Dechallenge:** The suspected drug was discontinued. Subsequent symptom improvement and trends in ALT, AST, bilirubin, and alkaline phosphatase should be documented before drawing a conclusion.

**Rechallenge:** Deliberate re-exposure is not recommended solely to confirm causality when drug-induced liver injury is suspected, because recurrence may be more severe.

## 10. Clinical Recommendations and Safety Considerations

* Arrange appropriate clinical evaluation and serial liver function monitoring.
* Investigate alternative causes of jaundice and liver injury.
* Review all prescription medicines, over-the-counter products, supplements, and actual doses taken.
* Document the suspected reaction and report it through the applicable pharmacovigilance reporting pathway.
* Avoid re-exposure to the suspected medicine unless a specialist determines otherwise after an appropriate risk–benefit assessment.
* Educate the patient about seeking urgent medical attention for worsening jaundice, persistent vomiting, confusion, or other concerning symptoms.

## 11. Conclusion

This simulated case illustrates how a pharmacovigilance professional evaluates a suspected adverse drug reaction by reviewing the medication history, symptom timeline, laboratory findings, dechallenge information, and possible alternative explanations.

Amoxicillin–clavulanate is the primary suspected drug because the presentation is compatible with its recognized liver safety profile. However, a definitive causal conclusion cannot be made without additional clinical information and follow-up.
